The newest app on my shelf is [BrightRecorder](https://github.com/gi-os/BrightRecorder), and the fastest way to describe it is by what it refuses to be. It is not a voice recorder. There is no transcription, no trimming, no waveform editor, no file manager. It records rooms, streets, weather, rain on windows — and the only thing it does with them is let you find them again.

## Tapes, not files

Record a moment and it becomes a clip named for where you were and when. Clips live on tapes, and you keep more than one — a tape for a trip, a tape for the apartment, a tape for the year. The machine holds exactly one tape at a time, and you swipe through the rest on a shelf. Each tape gets a name and a pattern on its label, which is what color would be if this panel had any.

Under the hood a tape is a directory of WAV files and nothing more. The clip's timestamp and place are the filename. Rename the folder, you've renamed the tape. Plug the phone into a desktop and the whole collection reads as itself — no database, no export step, no app required to get your own recordings back. I keep coming back to that rule in these apps: the storage format should make the app optional.

## The wheel finally has its job

The part I'm happiest with: winding. Turn the brightness wheel and you wind through a tape, forwards or backwards, and you *hear it while you wind*, the way a real tape machine chatters while you scrub. Let go and it plays from where you landed.

I've put the wheel to work in most of my apps — scrolling, lens ring, brightness — but this is the first time it's felt less like an adaptation and more like the thing the hardware was born for. A physical wheel wants to shuttle tape. It's been waiting for this job since the phone shipped.

None of this is a new idea. It's the oldest audio interface there is, drawn in software. That's the whole appeal — a tape recorder was already a nearly perfect object, and the only modern thing this phone needed to add was the filename.
